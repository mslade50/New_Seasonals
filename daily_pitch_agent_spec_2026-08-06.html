Daily Pitch Agent — Build Specification

Written 2026-08-06 for handoff to a build agent. Author: Claude session with McKinley. Repo: New_Seasonals. Companion conventions referenced throughout: CLAUDE.md, the memory directory, scratch/ultracode_research/. Status of this doc: approved DIRECTION from McKinley; implementation details inside are the writing session's recommendations and the build agent may improve on them, except where a block is marked as a hard requirement.

1. What McKinley asked for, verbatim intent

A once-per-day, non-interactive delivery of exactly three trade ideas that combine novelty with the full knowledge of this repo: the strategy book, all research in scratch/ultracode_research/, the macro event calendar, seasonality machinery, earnings data, the risk-dial state, and 25 years of prices. He will read the three ideas, decide yes or no per idea, and place orders. Nothing else. His words that define the product:

  • "I want to be able to ping you, any time" evolved to: a finished daily delivery, no conversation.
  • "I want creativity and logic combined." Novel constructions, then empirically interrogated before delivery.
  • "You shouldn't just rip off my own trades because what is the point of that?"
  • "False positives are fine. I will filter the ideas myself."
  • Ideas must ALWAYS have a defined entry and a defined exit (grammar in section 4).
  • "If that means you need to spin up multiple agents to verify or pushback on the ideas, that is fine but I want the ideas polished and finished by the time they make it to me."
Explicit non-goals. (1) NOT a systematic activation engine. An earlier design (pre-registered "card library" that code activates) was proposed and McKinley rejected it: "I don't want this at all. I don't want it to be systematic. We have already solved that." The strategy book already IS the systematic layer. (2) NOT interactive. No follow-up dialogue is assumed or required for the ideas to be complete. (3) NOT a site tab. The site's Ideas tab was deliberately removed 2026-07-28 (see memory seasonal-signals-screener and the CLAUDE.md site section); do not resurrect it. (4) NO order is ever placed without an explicit per-idea approval (section 7).

3. The core loop: invent, then falsify, then polish

This is the heart of the product and the reason it is an agent pipeline rather than a script. The proven prototype is the 2026-08-06 session itself (see scratch/ultracode_research/event_seasonality_sweep_2026-08-06.md and its four addenda): novel constructions like the gold-vs-silver divergence around August NFP, the DX-futures dollar fade, the SVXY-instead-of-short-UVXY expression, and the September inversion of the post-opex vol crush were each invented from repo context and then confirmed or killed with a five-minute empirical check against the repo's own data. The pipeline industrializes exactly that loop.

Stage B — Ideation (main agent, wide net)

Generate 8 to 12 raw candidates. Force breadth through named novelty axes; every batch must draw from at least four different axes:

  1. Instrument translation: a known effect expressed through a better vehicle (the SVXY and DX precedents).
  2. Interaction cells: event x seasonal x cycle-year x tape-state combinations no study has opened (the repo's studies are the periodic table; the compounds are mostly unexplored).
  3. Relative value: long X vs short Y where research only ever examined the legs alone (gold vs silver into August NFP was born this way).
  4. Inversion hunting: conditions under which a documented edge flips sign (September flipping the post-opex vol crush; midterm years flipping the pre-FOMC drift).
  5. Historical analogues: nearest-neighbor tapes to today (return/vol/breadth profile) and what worked in the following week, with honest N.
  6. Flow mechanics: dealer gamma around expiries, futures roll, month-end rebalancing, index adds, buyback windows as thesis engines.
  7. Event fingerprints: the cross-asset tables (like the midterm-August-NFP fingerprint) applied to whatever the calendar serves up in the horizon.
Anti-rip-off rule (hard requirement). An idea that is materially a STRATEGY_BOOK trade, an event-sleeve trade, or a seasonal-sheet ticket is dead on arrival unless it adds a genuine twist (different instrument, different leg structure, opposite side, meaningfully different window), and the write-up must name the overlap. The scanner already trades the book; repeating it has zero value to McKinley.

Stage C — Falsification fan-out (subagents, adversarial)

Each surviving candidate is handed to a checking agent whose brief is to KILL it. Checks are fast empirical interrogations, not backtests:

  • Does the claimed pattern exist in the data at all? Run the 5-minute study (window returns around the anchor, proper controls like the all-events baseline and the unconditional drift of the instrument; the sweep scripts in scratch/ are the pattern to imitate).
  • What is N, the worst window, and the era stability (did it die after 2018)?
  • Does it collide with the NEGATIVE-results registry (section 5)?
  • Does the book already hold correlated exposure, and does the idea survive that overlap disclosed?
  • Cost sanity: spread/carry of the vehicle vs the edge magnitude (the UUP lesson: 6 bps of edge cannot pay ETF drag; the same effect in DX futures was fine).
  • Tomorrow-specific tail risk: is a known vol-event inside the window (the last three August NFPs spiked VIX +7%, +26%, +22%)?

Recommended shape: 2-3 verifier agents in parallel, each owning 3-4 ideas, writing small throwaway scripts under scratch/pitch_checks/ (dated subfolder). A final red-team pass reviews the survivors together and may kill more. Ideas killed at this stage are logged with reasons (section 8), not shown to McKinley. The Workflow tool (or plain parallel subagents) is the natural harness; the build agent may choose the mechanism, but the CONTRACT is a hard requirement: every delivered idea must include falsification evidence computed fresh that morning, and at least one named consideration that could have killed it but did not.

Stage D — Compose and polish (main agent)

Select the best three by risk/reward over the stated horizon, graded honestly (section 5), written in the fixed template (section 4), in McKinley's prose taste: no AI tells, no em dashes, no hedging filler (memory writing-style-no-ai-tells). Each idea ends with an exact, copyable order specification.

4. The idea grammar (hard requirement)

Every idea, every day, fits this template. Entries and exits come only from these vocabularies:

ENTRY:  MOO | MOC | limit at OPEN +/- k*ATR | limit at CLOSE +/- k*ATR
        (k stated; ATR = 14d Wilder on the traded instrument; limit ideas state
         a fill window, e.g. "good for tomorrow only")
EXIT:   time stop (N trading days, exit MOC/MOO) | target +k*ATR | stop -k*ATR |
        event-anchored date (e.g. "exit at the close before the FOMC decision")
        At least a time stop is ALWAYS present; stop/target optional per idea.

Card layout per idea:

#1  [GRADE B]  Long GLD / short SLV into Friday's NFP        horizon: 5 td
    ENTRY: paired MOC today, dollar-neutral legs
    EXIT:  time stop 5 td (MOC), no price stop (spread trade, sized to risk)
    SIZE:  30 bps NAV risk (spread ATR basis)
    THESIS: 3-5 sentences. Variant perception (what the market prices vs what
            the evidence says) and who is on the other side / why it exists.
    EVIDENCE: the fresh numbers with N, the control, the era note. One table max.
    WHAT KILLS IT: the intraweek observation that would invalidate the thesis.
    OVERLAP: what the book/sleeve already holds that correlates, if anything.

5. Honesty machinery (hard requirements)

Evidence grades, printed on every idea

GradeMeaning
AEffect with N >= 50, |t| >= 2.5, holds across eras, verified fresh today.
BReal pattern, moderate sample (N 15-50) or single-era, verified fresh today.
CContext-grade: small-N fingerprint or analogue reasoning (N < 15). At most ONE grade-C idea per day, and its label must say "context, not statistics".

The negative-results registry

Build one flat file (suggested data/pitch_negative_registry.md) seeded from the repo's recorded dead ends, and REQUIRE stage C to check candidates against it. Seed contents: dial-conditioned caps/throttles, put hedges and VXX proxies, naked UVXY shorts at size, post-CPI vol crush (dead after 2018), post-NFP/post-FOMC/VIX-expiry vol cells, SVXY as a T1 leg, UUP as a dollar vehicle, famous calendar factor cells arbitraged post-2013, marginal-fill decompositions (memory no-marginal-fill-decompositions), notional-denominated caps (memory no-notional-caps), Heston-Sadka on liquid names, sub-50 sizing ramps, 21d "fast confirm". Each entry cites its study. The registry grows: every pitch-pipeline kill with a reusable lesson gets appended.

Repetition control

An idea pitched and declined is not re-pitched within 10 trading days unless its activating conditions have materially changed, and the write-up must then say what changed. The journal (section 8) enforces this.

6. Delivery: the email

Standalone HTML email, subject Daily Pitch — YYYY-MM-DD — 3 ideas, sent via the same SMTP creds/env the risk report uses (EMAIL_USER/EMAIL_PASS pattern). Layout: the three cards from section 4, then a compact footer: one line per killed finalist ("killed: short EWZ post-opex — fails era check, edge died 2019"), the current scoreboard line (section 8), and the standard disclaimers of grade meanings. Plain HTML matching the house email style (the scan email's card aesthetic). No links requiring interaction; the email IS the product.

7. The y/n approval loop

McKinley wants to approve and place orders with minimal friction, without the pipeline ever placing anything autonomously. Recommended mechanism, reusing house plumbing:

  1. The pipeline writes a Pitch tab in the Trade_Signals_Log workbook alongside the email: one row per idea with the full order spec (Ticker/Contract, Action, Entry_Type, Entry_Offset_ATR, Quantity, Stop_ATR, Target_ATR, Time_Exit_Date, Horizon, Grade) plus an empty Approve column. Rows carry Execute_On = today and expire at the next run (clear+rewrite, same convention as every other staging tab).
  2. McKinley types Y in the Approve cell for the ideas he wants (from phone or desktop; Sheets is already his approval surface for Manual_Limit pins).
  3. A small pre-market runner (pattern: event_moo.py — fail-closed validation, activation flag, placement journal, clientId of its own) reads Approve == Y rows at ~9:00 and stages/places equity+ETF orders through the existing machinery. Blank or anything other than Y places nothing. Futures rows are NOT auto-placed in v1 (the futures gateway is designed but not shipped, see EXECUTION_GATEWAY_FUTURES_DESIGN.md); the email prints the exact contract spec for manual entry and the tab row is marked Manual_Only.
Hard requirement: the approval default is NO. A pipeline failure, a malformed row, a stale Execute_On, or an ambiguous Approve value must all place nothing, loudly. Follow the trend_moo/event_moo validation conventions exactly (schema check across the whole basket before any broker contact, notional cap, row cap, dedup journal, OPG/MOC cutoffs).

8. The pitch journal and scoreboard

Because "false positives are fine" only stays fine if they are counted. Hard requirement.

  • Journal: append-only file (suggested data/pitch_journal.parquet or JSONL): date, all pitched ideas (full spec + grade + thesis hash), all killed finalists with reasons, McKinley's Approve values once known, and later the outcome.
  • Outcome grading: a nightly job replays each pitched idea's DEFINED entry/exit against master_prices exactly as specced (this is why the grammar is mandatory) and books the hypothetical R and PnL, filled-or-not for limit entries. Both approved and declined ideas get graded, so the scoreboard also measures McKinley's filter.
  • Scoreboard: one footer line in the daily email (rolling 60d: pitched N, hit rate by grade, avg R, approved-vs-declined performance gap) and a monthly section with the same by idea-class. If the creative layer is not earning its keep, this is where it shows, and the product should be killed or retuned on that evidence like any other strategy.

9. Runtime and scheduling

Recommendation: a scheduled headless Claude Code run on McKinley's machine (Task Scheduler, weekdays ~7:00 AM ET, after the 4:47 scan chain, before the 9:00 approval runner), in this repo, with a purpose-built skill (e.g. /daily-pitch) containing the stage A-D process. Rationale: full repo + data access, the memory directory, the existing SMTP/Sheets creds, and the same machine already runs four scheduled trading tasks. Alternative if local reliability is a concern: a scheduled cloud agent, but it must then solve R2/Sheets/master_prices access (the daily_screener GHA pattern shows how). Token budget note: stages B-D with 3-4 subagent verifications is a heavy run; budget accordingly and do not silently truncate the falsification stage to save tokens; fewer, better-checked ideas beat unchecked ones (but the delivery is still always three).
