# Archetype Underwriting Frameworks

## Route on two dimensions

Assign one primary business-model lane to determine accounting, KPIs, valuation, and downside. Assign one or more idea archetypes to explain why the security may be mispriced now.

Do not rank economically incomparable lanes on one universal composite. Compare within a lane first, then prioritize across lanes using decision readiness, valuation skew, evidence path, and portfolio usefulness.

## Select the idea archetype

| Archetype | Require | Reject first when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quality compounder at fair expectations | Durable incremental returns, per-share compounding, runway, clean cash conversion, rational capital allocation | Valuation already capitalizes the runway, reinvestment is fading, or dilution absorbs growth |
| Derated quality / temporary dislocation | Intact economics, bounded issue, reset expectations, identifiable repair evidence | The issue is structural, management credibility is broken, or estimates have not reset |
| Revision inflection | Leading KPI improvement, broad/continuing estimate path, reasonable valuation, evidence window | The revision is one-time, one-analyst, commodity/FX/tax-driven, or price moved more than estimates |
| Self-help / margin improvement | Measurable actions, credible bridge, controllable cost base, protected revenue | Savings already sit in consensus, revenue erosion offsets savings, or restructuring repeats indefinitely |
| Capital-allocation catalyst | Sustainable excess cash, governance permission, explicit deployment path | Cash is cyclical, trapped, needed for refinancing, or management is an empire builder |
| Cyclical normalization | Mid-cycle volume/margin/cash flow, cost position, balance-sheet endurance | Peak earnings are used as normal, trough lasts beyond liquidity, or structural demand changed |
| Hidden asset / SOTP | Segment disclosure, credible asset values, leakage analysis, closing mechanism | Discount is deserved, taxes/leakage consume value, or no catalyst exists |
| Post-event overreaction | Price move exceeds durable estimate/value change, source-backed misread, repair path | The event changes long-run economics or management credibility more than headline estimates show |

## Apply the business-model lane

### Recurring revenue, software, and subscription

Analyze ARR/RPO, gross and net retention, new-logo growth, customer concentration, gross margin, CAC payback, sales efficiency, mature operating margin, SBC-adjusted FCF, and diluted shares. Value with mature-state cash flow, EV/revenue or EV/gross-profit cross-checks, and reverse expectations. Reject deferred-revenue timing, underinvestment, SBC, acquisition, or AI narrative false positives.

### Consumer product, retail, restaurant, and unit growth

Separate traffic/volume from price/mix. Analyze same-store sales, frequency, loyalty, AUV, unit growth, four-wall returns, gross margin, promotions, inventory/markdowns, wage/input costs, leases, and credit exposure. Value on normalized margins, unit economics, lease-adjusted metrics, and FCF. Reject price-only comps, temporary freight/input relief, or under-reserved lease/capex needs.

### Transaction networks, payments, and marketplaces

Analyze volume, frequency, take rate, active buyers/sellers, cohorts, retention, CAC/LTV, contribution margin, loss/fraud/chargebacks, network density, and regulation. Value through gross profit and mature cash economics. Reject volume without take-rate durability, subsidized growth, or apparent network effects without retention.

### Industrials, semiconductors, and capital-intensive cyclicals

Analyze organic orders, backlog quality/conversion, book-to-bill, capacity/utilization, inventory/channel health, price/cost, aftermarket mix, working capital, maintenance versus growth capex, customer/end-market concentration, and mid-cycle margins. Value on mid-cycle earnings and FCF. Reject late-cycle backlog, double ordering, peak margins, or deferred maintenance.

### Commodity producers, miners, and E&P

Analyze reserve/mine life, grade/decline, cost-curve position, AISC/lifting cost, sustaining capex, committed growth capex, hedges, jurisdiction, permitting, balance sheet, and capital-return policy. Value with NAV and FCF under conservative and spot commodity assumptions. Reject spot-price capitalization, understated sustaining capex, or political/permitting fragility.

### Banks and lenders

Analyze P/TBV versus sustainable ROTCE, CET1, NIM, deposit beta/flows, loan growth, fee income, credit normalization, reserves, nonperformers, CRE/concentration, AOCI/duration, liquidity, and capital return. Never use generic EV/EBITDA or corporate FCF. Reject low P/E created by unsustainably low credit costs or temporary NIM.

### Insurance

Analyze combined/loss/expense ratios, reserve development, premium and pricing cycle, cat exposure, investment yield, capital adequacy, and book-value-per-share compounding. Value through reserve-adjusted book value, ROE spread, and normalized earnings. Reject reserve releases or investment gains presented as underwriting quality.

### Asset managers

Analyze AUM by strategy, net flows, fee rate, performance fees, investment performance, operating leverage, seeding obligations, distribution concentration, and capital return. Value on normalized fee-related earnings and cash generation. Reject market-beta AUM gains presented as organic growth.

### REITs

Analyze AFFO/share, same-store NOI, occupancy, leasing spreads, tenant concentration, lease duration, recurring capex, development commitments, debt maturities, floating-rate exposure, and refinancing. Value with NAV/implied cap rates and P/AFFO. Reject dividend-yield support, stale cap rates, or GAAP earnings as the core metric.

### Utilities and infrastructure

Analyze rate-base growth, allowed ROE, regulatory jurisdictions, capex plan, funding mix, equity issuance, rate cases, project execution, and catastrophe/liability risk. Value against growth-adjusted regulated peers and dividend coverage. Reject unfunded capex or regulatory lag hidden by headline EPS growth.

### Commercial pharma and healthcare products

Analyze product/procedure volumes, pricing/reimbursement, payer mix, patent cliffs, physician adoption, pipeline productivity, competition, and R&D economics. Value with patent-cliff-adjusted earnings and risk-adjusted pipeline/SOTP. Reject unmodeled loss of exclusivity or reimbursement pressure.

### Pre-commercial biotech

Use event-driven risk-adjusted NPV, trial design/endpoints, probability by development stage, competitive timing, regulatory path, IP, cash runway, and dilution. Do not apply a generic quality/FCF score. Require explicit binary downside and financing paths before review readiness.

### Conglomerates and special situations

Use segment-specific economics, SOTP, tax/leakage, capital allocation, legal structure, event probabilities, and timing. Route transaction-driven cases to `event-driven-analyzer`. Reject headline discounts without a credible closing mechanism.

## Underwrite every lane through the same PM questions

1. State the causal earnings engine.
2. Identify the denominator most likely to be misleading.
3. Distinguish business growth from per-share value creation.
4. Quantify what the current price implies.
5. State the specific variant wedge.
6. Identify the KPI and estimate path that monetizes the wedge.
7. Explain why the market may be right.
8. Explain the downside mechanism and balance-sheet path.
9. Define the next proof point and kill condition.
10. Assess portfolio novelty and correlated drivers.

## Integrate trend without substituting it for research

Use adjusted price history and classify:

- `GREEN`: price above a rising 200-day average with nonnegative 12-minus-1-month benchmark-relative return.
- `AMBER`: mixed evidence.
- `RED`: price below a falling 200-day average with negative relative return.

Require several confirming closes or a small volatility buffer before changing state. Use trend to change timing, confidence, and conditional sizing. Do not use it to prove company quality or mispricing. Treat a red-trend review as exceptional and require a hard catalyst/capitulation rationale. Use the 200-week average only as optional secular context for deep cyclicals and turnarounds.

## Ignore weak shortcuts

De-emphasize raw P/E, unadjusted EPS beats, generic moat language, founder status alone, product familiarity alone, management adjectives, price action as proof, precise DCF targets without driver support, and macro forecasts without a causal company transmission path.
