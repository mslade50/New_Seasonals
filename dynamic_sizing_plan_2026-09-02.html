Sizing Hierarchy Plan

A decision framework for making the book's sizing dynamic in the three places the evidence says dynamics live: adds inside a strategy, correlation between strategies, and the market factor the risk dial detects. It is built on growth-optimal sizing where that applies, and steps outside it where it does not.

1. What the book is, in growth terms

Every sizing argument starts from the growth-rate identity. For a fraction f of capital in a bet with excess mean μ and variance σ², compound growth is approximately

g(f) ≈ f·μ − ½·f²·σ² f* = μ/σ² g(f*) = μ²/(2σ²) = S²/2

where S is the Sharpe ratio. The book's daily mark-to-market series gives μ and σ directly, so the first question is where current sizing sits on that curve.

Two consequences shape everything below.

  • The book runs at about 6% of full Kelly. That far down the curve, growth is linear in f and the compounding tax is negligible. The Kelly argument therefore has nothing to say about the global multiplier; the global multiplier is a drawdown-appetite choice, which is what the 2026-07-16 GRM replay found empirically (Sharpe 1.89 to 1.83 across GRM 1.0 to 1.75). Section L4 replaces "appetite" with a stated probability rule.
  • Where Kelly does bite is relative allocation. Inside a fixed total, the growth-optimal split across bets is proportional to each bet's μ/σ² after accounting for correlation. That is the lever this plan works, at four levels of aggregation.

The 2016+ Sharpe of 2.67 is not a number to size to. It carries survivorship in the overflow tier, in-sample rule selection, and a flat-dollar basis. Section L5 turns that discount into a measured quantity; until measured, the plan uses a 50% haircut on μ wherever a number is sized to.

2. The framework: one statistic per level

The article's closing line, "size themes, not tickers; give each idea capital only for what it adds beyond the book", is a hierarchy. The version of it that survives contact with this book has six levels. Each level owns exactly one question and one statistic, and a control belongs at the lowest level where its statistic moves. That rule is what stopped this study from proposing a book-level throttle: the thing that changes with the dial is not book variance, it is book composition, so the control belongs at L3, not L4.

L0

Leg: the unit of risk keep

Question. Does one ATR of risk buy the same expected R in a quiet name as in a wild one?

The book sizes shares as risk_bps × NAV / (stop_atr × ATR). That is inverse-volatility sizing, one power of σ, while Kelly wants μ/σ², two powers. They coincide only if dollar edge itself scales with ATR, which it does when targets and stops are in ATR units and the R distribution does not depend on the vol level. That second condition is testable and it holds:

L1

Strategy: base bps as growth efficiency ship, capped

Question. Holding the book's total risk fixed, is risk distributed across strategies in proportion to what each one earns per unit of growth-relevant risk, once tails, concurrency and estimation error are counted?

This is the level where the article's Rung 1 and Rung 6 both bind, and where a one-table answer is wrong in three separate ways: the quadratic Kelly form misprices skew, the per-trade view ignores that a strategy's legs overlap, and any point estimate from 24 to 2,000 trades is mostly noise. Each is handled in turn, then the two independent routes to an allocation are compared, and the allocation is tested out of sample before anything is recommended.

The article's "distrust your μ" is right, but the thing to distrust most in this book is the left tail of a small sample, and it cuts the index dip-buys, not the breakouts.

1b. The concurrency term

A per-trade optimum assumes the trades happen one at a time. They do not: a strategy's legs overlap, and when they overlap with correlation ρ the growth-optimal size per leg falls by the Rung 5 factor. Using the mean number of simultaneous legs on active days and the implied ρ from L2's sleeve-variance test:

1d. Out of sample: does adopting the weights actually help?

A walk-forward re-fits the daily-route weights each January on all data through the prior December and applies them to the following year, 2014 to 2026, with every weight set scaled so that its in-sample book volatility equals the equal-weight book's. Four variants are tested: a half-blend toward the expanding-window weights (w_used = 0.5·w + 0.5), the same with 5-year and 3-year half-lives on the moments, and a tail-aware allocation proportional to each strategy's PnL per unit of worst-day loss (1e below). The gate was written before the run: adopt only if PnL/maxDD improves by more than 10% out of sample and no held-out year loses more than 15% of the equal-weight PnL.

Second, era stability. The multiplier scales the strategy's base bps only and composes with every existing overlay (frag bands, recency ladder, earnings override, derates), which is what keeps OLV's 1.29 from re-opening the stack question: its ladder and ticker cap still apply on top. Confirmation gate before it goes live: the engine replay via process_signals_fast(risk_multipliers=…) must reproduce at least half of the walk-forward PnL gain with maxDD no worse by more than one point, because the daily-series overlay cannot see cap interactions (a raised strategy hits its 250 bps daily cap more often, a cut one less). The annual re-fit is mechanical and the numbers go in the commit message; the cap means no January can move a strategy more than 40% from base in either direction.

What this does not do: it does not touch GRM (total in-sample vol is held), it does not size by dial (that is L3), and it does not rescue a strategy whose edge has gone. A strategy whose expanding-window weight falls below 0.6 for two consecutive years is a retirement question, not a sizing one.

L2

Adds: the n-th leg of a stack shipreject

Question. When a strategy adds its n-th concurrent leg, is that a new independent bet, a scaled-up version of the same bet, or a worse version of it?

The article's Rung 4 says a sleeve of look-alikes at pairwise correlation ρ deserves at most μ/(σ²ρ) in total, however many names. The two ingredients are observable from the sleeve's own daily PnL: with n legs of equal size, sleeve variance is nσ²(1+(n−1)ρ), so the ratio of the sleeve's daily PnL sd to sd₁√n identifies ρ, and the per-leg mean identifies whether the marginal leg carries edge.

Three distinct answers come out of one table.

OLV stacks are one bet, and a better one

Variance scales like n, not √n: a deep OLV stack behaves as a single position with ρ near 1, because the legs cluster in one sector washout (the June 2026 oil names, the August 2026 airlines). But the per-leg mean rises with depth, 67 to 320 dollars a day, and at the trade level legs entered with six or more OLV names open average 1.34R against 0.55R for a solo entry. With ρ = 1 the stack's Kelly-optimal total is μ_stack/σ²_stack, which does not grow with n at all when per-leg stats are constant; here per-leg μ/σ² rises about five-fold from depth 1 to depth 8, so the optimal stack total does rise, roughly five-fold, while the book's linear stacking rises eight-fold. Depth 7+ is therefore modestly over the Kelly shape and depth 2 to 6 is about right.

The math says hedge, not cut

Write the book's covariance as one market factor plus idiosyncratic risk, Σ = D + σ²ₘ ββᵀ, and allow a costless index future as an extra asset with mean μₘ. Solving w = Σ⁻¹μ with the future included separates into two pieces:

idiosyncratic book: w_idio = D⁻¹ μ_idio (Kelly on what the market cannot replicate) market position: w_m = μₘ/σ²ₘ − βᵀ w_idio (own Kelly on the index, minus the book's embedded beta)

The second line is the whole argument. The optimal index position is the book's embedded beta, hedged out, plus whatever the index deserves on its own. In calm tape μₘ is positive (SPY +22.6% annualised in the < 30 bucket, 2016+) and the residual beta is welcome. At dial 65 to 80 the conditional SPY mean is −28.9% annualised: the term flips sign and the optimum is to short the embedded beta and then some. A scalar cut on the book does neither of these things; it shrinks the idiosyncratic Kelly book, where the edge is, by the same factor as the market exposure it was trying to remove. That is why every throttle in Section 4 loses and the hedge does not.

The full-sample improvement is small because the armed buckets are 13% of days; inside them it is large. Two caveats keep this at pre-registration rather than ship, and both are specific to this book.

  • The dial was calibrated on SPY forward returns. Its signal weights are diff_mean edges on SPY. A negative conditional SPY mean at high readings is therefore partly what the dial was fit to produce, and the hedge test inherits that lookahead. The July PIT re-estimate (scratch/pit_reestimate.py) exists precisely to remove it; the hedge must be scored on those vintage-lagged weights before it is believed.
  • Episode count. The 65+ buckets are 133 days across roughly five episodes (Q4 2018, early 2020, 2021-12, 2022, 2026-08), and the last one is live and was hedged by hand. Any decision rule has to be episode-clustered.

Decision: pre-register a dial-armed book beta hedge; paper-track; ship only on the PIT result.

Instrument MES/ES sized to β̂ × NAV, armed when the 10d-MA 63d dial crosses the threshold and released when it falls back below with a 5-point hysteresis; β̂ from the flat-basis book MTM on SPY, 63 days, lag-1, refreshed daily. This is the systematic form of the August 2026 manual hedge, and the one dial-conditioned control in this study that acts on the quantity the dial actually predicts. Protocol in Section 6.

L4

Book: the survivable total rule

Question. What global multiplier is survivable, stated as a probability rather than a feeling?

With the book at 6% of Kelly, growth theory is silent on GRM and the binding constraint is drawdown. The drawdown-constrained growth literature (Grossman and Zhou) makes exposure a function of the running drawdown; that only pays if drawdowns predict anything, and here they do not: conditional on the book being 5 to 10% below its 252-day high, the next 21 days average +25% annualised on 10% vol, indistinguishable from any other state. So the plan uses the static version of the constraint: a stationary block bootstrap of the 2016+ daily series (mean block 10 sessions, 3,000 one-year paths) gives the probability that a year's maximum drawdown exceeds a threshold, at each multiple of current sizing and each haircut on the daily mean.

The rule replaces the appetite reading with three declared numbers (drawdown threshold, probability budget, haircut). Changing any of them is a decision that gets written down; the frontier script recomputes the answer. When L5 delivers a measured haircut the 50% placeholder is replaced by it, which is the only path by which GRM rises. Book-level dynamic scaling of any kind is not part of the rule (Section 4).

L5

Measurement: the haircut, measured build

Question. How much of the ledger's edge shows up in fills?

The article's strongest practical claim is that the output of any sizing formula is an order of magnitude more sensitive to μ than to ρ. The book has the data to measure its own μ discount: verify_fills.py writes fill status to Trade_Signals_Log, and the Trade Log tab holds executions for both accounts. Matched trade by trade, the ratio of realised live R to ledger R (and the fill rate of staged limits) is the haircut that L1's shrinkage and L4's frontier should use instead of 50%.

Decision: build the live-vs-ledger reconciliation as a scheduled script and publish the ratio on the site's Execution tab.

Until at least 150 matched trades exist, the plan's haircut stays at 50%. Every later sizing study cites the measured number and its date.

4. The negative results, and why they are load-bearing

The natural first idea for "dynamic sizing from the dial" is a scalar on the book. The reason is structural, not a matter of the instrument. Book variance is driven by whether positions are open, and positions open when signals fire, and signal density is itself the edge: the best single predictor of next-month book vol is the trailing five-day signal count (Spearman 0.24), and the same variable predicts next-month book mean just as well (0.22). Anything that scales the book down when it is about to be volatile scales it down when it is about to make money. The dial itself predicts neither (0.13 and 0.10; R² 1% and 0.2%).

These rows are the institutional memory the July studies already recorded (throttle t = −0.23, taper −11.4R) reproduced in a different frame, and they should close the question for the book level. The dial's information survives at L3 because there it is not used to scale anything: it is used to decide the sign of a factor position.

6. Pre-registration protocols

P1. Dial-armed book beta hedge

  1. Series. Flat-basis daily book MTM from the ledger build; SPY total return from master_prices. Dial: 10d MA of the 63d column, lag-1. Two dial vintages scored separately: current weights (the live series) and the PIT vintage-lagged weights from scratch/pit_reestimate.py. The PIT vintage is primary.
  2. β̂. 63-session OLS of book return on SPY, lag-1, clipped [−1, 2]. Sensitivity: 42 and 126 sessions.
  3. Arming. Primary threshold 50, release at 45. Sensitivity: 65/60. Hedge PnL = −β̂ × SPY return × NAV on armed days, less 2 bps round-trip per arm event for futures friction.
  4. Decision statistic. Hedge PnL summed per armed episode (episodes separated by ≥ 21 unarmed sessions); episode-clustered t of mean episode PnL; full-sample equal-vol Sharpe of hedged vs unhedged; LOYO.
  5. Ship rule. On the PIT vintage: mean armed-episode hedge PnL > 0 with clustered t ≥ 1.0, full-sample equal-vol Sharpe ≥ unhedged, and at least 6 of 10 LOYO years not worse. Otherwise parked, with the table filed.
  6. Paper period. One full armed episode logged on the hedge panel before live orders. Live mechanics: MES contracts = β̂ × NAV / (50 × ES price), native MOC entry per the 2026-08-21 encoding rule, quarterly roll on the panel's roll-off schedule.

P2. Exemption re-test at dial 65

  1. Cell: trades of OLV, LT Trend ST OS and OVS with signal-date PIT dial ≥ 65, 2018+ (PIT weights start there).
  2. Statistic: avgR difference vs the same strategy at < 50, episode-clustered t (episodes = runs of ≥ 65 separated by 21 sessions), LOYO floor.
  3. Rule: a band [[65, 999, 0.5]] is adopted for a strategy only if its clustered t ≤ −2.0 on PIT weights AND the effect survives dropping the best episode. If P1 ships first, the test is run on β-hedged trade returns.

P3. Base-bps tilt, engine confirmation

The walk-forward in L1d already met the pre-stated gate (PnL/maxDD +10%, no held-out year losing > 15%). Confirmation before go-live: run process_signals_fast(risk_multipliers=…) with the shipping multipliers on the full ledger (flat basis), compare to the unmodified ledger. Pass if the engine reproduces at least half of the walk-forward's annual PnL gain (≥ +2 points of NAV a year over 2014-2026) with maxDD no worse by more than one point and the worst 21-day window no worse by more than 10%. Fail means the cap interactions ate the gain; the multipliers are then re-derived with the per-strategy 250 bps daily cap inside the objective (a second walk-forward on engine output, not the daily series).

P4. 52wh Breakout dial-conditioned hold (exit rule, outside sizing scope)

Candidate: shorten the 63-day hold for open 52wh positions while the dial is ≥ 50 (e.g. exit MOC on the first close below the 10-day low once armed). Evidence to beat: 125 active days at ≥ 50 with Sharpe −2.2 while the entry gate at 30 already blocks new positions. PIT dial, episode-clustered, LOYO; ship only if the armed-window PnL improves with clustered t ≥ 1.5 and no calm-tape cost (the rule never fires below 50 by construction).

7. Caveats that bind every number here

  • Vintage. Dial rows before 2026-07-02 are the recompute vintage (drift up to about 7 points on the 63d column); the append-only PIT series has two months of history. Every bucket table above is current-weights, which is why the L3 decisions are pre-registrations.
  • Daily series end. The per-strategy MTM payload ends 2026-08-07; the ledger runs to 2026-09-01. The August 2026 high-dial episode is only partly inside the daily tests.
  • Overlay simulation. Scaling daily PnL by a lag-1 multiplier approximates scaling new entries; positions carry holds of 2 to 63 days, so a live overlay would respond more slowly than simulated. This biases the overlay tests in the overlays' favour, and they still lose.
  • Survivorship. The ledger trades only tickers alive today; overflow-tier avgR is an upper bound. L1 ratios for overflow-heavy strategies are less reliable than their N suggests.
  • Flat basis. All dollars are on the flat $750k basis; live NAV differs across accounts and time. Nothing here is sized in notional.
  • Small cells. Decisions 2 and 3 rest on 50 days and 23 trades. They are shipped as variance-only or appetite rules with re-exam triggers, not as evidenced edges, and the plan says so wherever they appear.
