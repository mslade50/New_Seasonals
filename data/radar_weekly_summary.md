## MP (MP Materials) — long, structural rerate

**Thesis.** DoD took a **15% equity stake ($400M preferred), set a $110/kg NdPr price floor, and pre-committed to 100% magnet offtake for 10 years**. Magnet sales from the Independence facility ramp H2 2026. This is not "a China headline that will fade" — this is US industrial policy on one ticker converting MP from a cyclical concentrate miner into a utility-like earnings profile with government-guaranteed pricing and demand.

**Variant perception.** The consensus print is that MP is another "rare-earth headline stock" that will chop with China escalation news. My variant view is that the $110/kg floor + 100% offtake for 10 years is a **structural earnings-power reset** that the sell-side FY27–FY29 curves have not adjusted for. Forward EPS: 0y $0.19 → +1y $1.03 (+441% growth), and the +1y high estimate is $1.80 (analyst count 14, so real coverage but wide dispersion — the market is guessing). The floor eliminates the historical Chinese-dumping tail risk that has kept multiples suppressed. That risk was ~30-40% of the fair-value discount on any rare-earth ex-China producer; it's gone.

**Who's on the other side.** Informed disagreement — traders holding MP from the pre-summer $80+ zone bailing into any bounce ("I'm never trading rare earths again"), plus systematic momentum-model exits after the June/July drawdown. RSI 27.1 confirms forced positioning-driven selling, not fundamental repricing. Bears will argue China can retaliate with an oxide flood; the counter is that the $110 floor plus DoD offtake means MP's economics are decoupled from spot NdPr in a way peers still aren't.

**The data.** Price $52.21 vs SMA200 $61.37 (down 48% from $100.25 52w high); market cap $9.3B; forward PE 50.8x looks expensive on 0y EPS but +1y EPS of $1.03 puts the +1y multiple ~50x with 441% next-year EPS growth stacked behind it — the ramp is not in the numerator yet. Volume ratio 0.95 — no capitulation flush yet, so entries should be scaled.

**What would change my mind.** A congressional action reversing/watering down the DoD equity+floor package (unlikely — bipartisan support); or a China-side action offering the US a rare-earth deal that removes the underlying policy motive.

**Variant perception.** Consensus reads the EO as political theater that will get watered down or ignored. Defense primes trade at premium multiples (RTX 25.8x forward for 9.6% EPS growth) partly BECAUSE of reliable capital returns. Take that lever away and the sector's yield-substitute crowd rotates out. **Who's on the other side.** Iran/Hormuz-escalation buyers reading RTX as generic "defense goes up on war" — they're right on the top line and wrong on the return-of-capital / multiple axis. A subset of yield-oriented institutional holders will only sell after the NDAA vote, which creates a two-step drop pattern: sell on the codification announcement, then sell again on the vote.

**The data.** Price $195.93; forward EPS 0y $6.92, +1y $7.58 (+9.6%); forward PE 25.8x; RSI 64.9; near 52w high $214.50. Pair against long defense-tech beneficiaries (AVAV, KTOS, LASR) that don't rely on buybacks and get the reallocation. **What would change my mind.** SASC drops the codification amendment from the NDAA markup (headline-driven, watchable). Or the White House swaps the EO for a softer alternative in exchange for defense-industry concessions.
