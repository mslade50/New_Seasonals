1. The answer in one page

The book is not drawdown-limited. It is broker-limited and composition-limited. Under every haircut and every tail model the growth curve is linear through three times current size, and the drawdowns that sizing would buy at two times current size (a median one-year drawdown of 10 to 15%, a 2 to 21% chance of a 20% year) are the ones you said you would accept. They are not what stops you. What stops you is a forced liquidation at 15:45 ET on one of the five to ten cluster days a decade when a long dip-buy cluster or an OVS-plus-3x-fade cluster pushes the portfolio-margin requirement past net liquidation, with no margin call and no cure period. That wall sits at 1.6x current size on the $750k base under plain portfolio-margin rates and at 1.3 to 1.5x on the live $632k account, before any of the up-size levers below add gross on exactly those days.

So the plan is: press every evidenced growth lever, and put the two boundary controls in first.

  • GRM 1.5 to 1.875 now, once a margin-feasibility guard is live in order_staging and IBKR's what-if has been run on the three historical peak-requirement books. Step to 2.25 when the guard has trimmed cleanly through one real cluster day or the index-ETF dip-buy legs have moved to micro futures. 2.5 to 3.0 only after both and a measured live haircut.
  • Allocation tilt across strategies by keep-adjusted inverse-covariance weights, half-blended and clipped to [0.7, 1.3]: cut 52wh, Weak Close, Sector BO, St OS Sznl, IOB; raise MonFri, the 3x fades, OLV, ATR Extended Gap Up, Monday Dip. Out of sample: PnL per unit of drawdown +20%, worst held-out year −4% versus today.
  • Within strategies: re-key the OLV ladder to sleeve depth so the appetite cut stops landing on the best legs; Weak Close and LT Trend at 0.8x solo, 1.2x on adds; OVS bottom-extremity signals at 0.7x; the OVS path-2 cap to 1.0%; index clone clamps. No 52wh depth rule, no correlation or sector cuts anywhere.
  • Flow: the per-strategy 250 bps cap breathes to 375 on a family's top-tercile signal-flow days, and the three mean-reverting families size 1.2x on those days. Never a size-down on high flow. Both switch off when the guard sees the requirement above 60% of NLV.
  • The dial arms a whole-book beta hedge in ES/MES at 50 (release 45), 126-day beta, paper-tracked for one episode then live. It is the one dial control that survives the point-in-time weights. No throttle, no VIX-armed anything, no calendar layer.
  • Haircut 40% on the ledger's edge, per-strategy keeps from 0.31 to 0.78, pilots capped at half their own estimate, commissions and exit spreads added to the engine, and a broker-fill reconciliation started today.

2. The framework: three walls, not one appetite

The first plan set the global multiplier by a probability rule on drawdown. You asked for that constraint to be removed and for the math to say where size should go. The math says three things, and the order matters.

g(m) = 252 · E[ log(1 + m·(r − h·μ)) ] m = multiple of current sizing, h = haircut on the daily mean 2003+ series, h = 0.40: g(1) 12.9% g(1.5) 18.8% g(2) 24.5% g(3) 34.8% m* ≈ 10.3 2016+ series, h = 0.40: g(1) 19.3% g(1.5) 28.4% g(2) 37.0% g(3) 53.3% m* ≈ 12.8 Curvature through m = 3: under 1 point on every window and haircut. Single-day ruin from the return distribution alone: m ≈ 17 (empirical) to 6.5 to 9 (GPD-extrapolated tails). None of it is reachable.
  1. Growth theory is silent in the feasible range. At 40% haircut the book sits at 8 to 13% of its own Kelly, and even the pessimistic 71% haircut leaves the argmax at the top of any grid you can physically trade. Thorp's and Ziemba's practitioner band of a quarter to a half of honest Kelly translates to two to four times current size. Nothing in the sizing literature argues against a step; it argues for one.
  2. The first real wall is portfolio margin. The book sizes ATR risk with no notional dimension, so low-ATR instruments carry enormous notional per unit of risk: SPY over 50% of NAV on 89 days since 2016, gross open notional p95 159%, p99 254%, max 418% of NAV. IBKR's TIMS requirement is on gross (no long/short offset for stocks), Soft Edge Margin expires at 15:45 ET, and liquidation is automated with no call. The historical maximum-requirement day needs 61% of the $750k base at plain rates (15% stock, 8% broad index, 45% 3x ETF), so the wall is m 1.60 on the base and 1.34 on the live NLV. Under a 30% concentration add-on the current book is already at 89% on that day; under rules-based 90% margin on short 3x ETFs, 99%.
  3. The second wall is composition. Every evidenced lever in this study is an up-size on the same legs on the same days: the cluster days. Stack them as the single-overlay studies proposed and the 2016+ maximum drawdown moves from 7.3% to 11.7%, with a new worst episode (June to July 2026, OLV) that the current book barely noticed. The levers have to be composed with a cap on their product and switched off when the first wall is near.

The drawdown distribution is disclosed at every step so the choice is made with open eyes, but it no longer constrains. What constrains is a rule with three inputs that can each be measured: the growth curve's curvature, the projected margin requirement on live NLV, and a single-day gap bound on open ATR risk.

3. L4 Book: the global multiplier and the sizing base ship, gated

Rule. GRM is the largest value such that: (i) growth is within 1 point of linear at the current haircut; (ii) the historical p99 requirement day projects below 70% of live NLV and the maximum day below 100% of the sizing base at plain PM rates, with rules-based 3x and 30% concentration reported as sensitivities; (iii) the 99.9th-percentile stress multiple times p99 open ATR risk times m stays under 15% of NAV. Drawdown tables are disclosed, not constrained. Re-derived quarterly and whenever NLV moves 20%.

Today: (i) passes at any m below 3; (ii) passes at m 1.25 (GRM 1.875) with the guard live and reliefs off on guard days (max day 71% of base, 84% of NLV) and fails at m 1.5 on live NLV (100%); (iii) passes at m 1.5 (book k99.9 1.28 × p99 open risk 641 bps × 1.5 = 12.3%). So GRM 1.875 now; 2.25 when either NLV reaches about $840k, or the index-ETF legs route to futures (which lifts the plain-PM wall to about m 2.3), or the guard has converted one real cluster day into a planned trim.

Why 1.875 now and not 2.25

Two of the four plans said 2.25 immediately; the skeptic and the practitioner said 1.875. The practitioner wins on the arithmetic that only he ran: with the levers composed, the peak-requirement day at 2.25 lands at 100% of the live NLV even with reliefs off, and the rules-based reading of short 3x margin (which IBKR applies to some client categories and has not been checked on this account) puts the current book at the wall already. A 15:45 auto-liquidation on a cluster day is a ruin channel, not a drawdown, and it is cheap to remove: the guard is a projection in order_staging and the what-if is an afternoon in Risk Navigator. The step to 2.25 is written as a rule with observable triggers, not deferred to judgement.

The margin guard ship first

Before the 9:31 chain, project the day's requirement on live NLV: open book plus every staged entry at full size, at 15% single stock, 8% broad index, 10% small-cap index, 45% 3x long (90% short until the what-if says otherwise), per-share minimums on sub-$16.67 shorts. Above 70%, scale the day's new entries pro rata to 70%; existing positions are never touched. Above 60%, the flow up-size and cap relief are off for the day. A 15:30 ET re-check on live marks alarms above 90%. It binds on 0.5 days a year at GRM 1.875 and about 2.6 at 3.0. It is the one control in this plan that reads notional, and it is not a risk rule: it is the broker's constraint made visible a day early so the trim is pro rata instead of the broker's choice of which position to sell.

Sizing base keep, with a written switch

The flat $750k base stays while live NLV is below it. A switch to live NAV today is a 16% risk cut (632/750), not a growth step, and the live-NLV margin wall is already the tighter one. When NLV exceeds $750k at a quarter end, move to half-compounding: base = max(prior base, 0.5 × 750k + 0.5 × NLV), reset quarterly, never below the prior quarter. Over ten years the bootstrap median terminal multiple is 4.3x flat, 9.0x half-compounding, 25.6x full, with identical percent-of-peak drawdowns and dollar drawdowns growing at half the full-compounding rate. Full compounding only if the measured live haircut comes in under 25%. The risk architect's 75%-of-base downside floor is not adopted: it is a drawdown-triggered cut in disguise, the book's PnL is negatively autocorrelated (which makes such cuts expectancy-negative), and the guard already tightens automatically as NLV falls because it is denominated on live NLV.

Per-strategy 250 bps cap

Fixed in effective bps through GRM 2.25 (it costs about one point of growth and alone bounds the worst single day at −$44k versus −$118k uncapped). The two relaxations are the flow-aware relief (section 7) and nothing else. Scaling the cap with GRM becomes an explicit second decision only past 3.0, where it would otherwise saturate the realised multiple near 4.6x.

Overflow tier does not take the step ship

The overflow-universe long strategies (OLV, LT Trend ST OS, St OS Sznl, 52wh) keep today's effective bps: their OVERFLOW_RISK_OVERRIDES nominals are scaled by 1.5/1.875 so the GRM step routes entirely to the liquid tier, the index legs and the short book. Overflow longs carry a survivorship keep of 0.85, are not more profitable than their liquid twins in any shared strategy (OLV 0.68 vs 0.97, LT Trend 0.29 vs 0.40, St OS 0.18 vs 1.02), cost twice the impact, and are the only positions already at 1 to 2.7% of ADV at p90. A participation rule of 1% of 21-day median dollar ADV per order (0.4% for strategies under 0.4R) with a hard refusal at 5% is written alongside.

6. L2 Adds, stacks, clones and quality tiers

The within-strategy study reversed the first plan's instinct in one important way: across OLV, OVS, Weak Close, LT Trend and the 3x ETF fade, the marginal add is the higher-edge leg, not a clone. Legs entered while the strategy already has positions open average more R than solo legs (OLV 0.48 solo to 1.18 at 13+ open; OVS 0.20 to 0.77; Weak Close 0.21 to 0.60; LT Trend 0.22 to 0.63), same-sector and same-ticker adds are better still, and trailing-63-day correlation with the open names predicts nothing about a leg's R in any strategy. At 6 to 13% of Kelly the growth-rate variance tax on a marginal leg is second order, so every add rule that cuts the n-th leg for being the n-th leg cut the best legs at equal risk: Kelly-concurrency 1/(1+nρ) rules, variance parity, sleeve caps, sector cuts, same-ticker cuts, stack-age cuts, all lose. The only negative marginal cell in the book is 52wh at six or more open legs, and only in the 2010+ era.

7. Flow: the one time-varying state that sizes ship, gated by the guard

Signal flow was the principal's question and it is the study's clearest positive. Trailing five-session raw candidate counts within a family forecast the next trades' expectancy in every mean-reverting family, and the sign is unambiguous: high flow is a size-up state. The "crowded selloff" reading is wrong for this book.

Two rules, one gate.

Cap relief. The per-strategy cap is 375 bps instead of 250 for every strategy in a family on any day the family's trailing-5d raw candidate count (counted from the same filter masks daily_scan evaluates, before earnings blackouts and T+1 gates) is at or above its threshold: dip_buy 6, oversold_hold 7, short_fade 104. The cap binds on 21% of trades and on exactly the good days (cap-bound OVS avgR 0.62 vs 0.20 unbound; Weak Close 0.96 vs 0.24). Unfitted replay 2010 to 2026: book PnL +3.8%, Sharpe 2.199 to 2.222, maxDD and worst-21d byte-identical. Never ×99: full removal takes short_fade's Sharpe from 1.18 to 0.93 and its maxDD from −$44k to −$127k. Breakouts excluded (the cap never binds there).

Up-size. A new sizing step after the frag band and before the OLV ladder: 1.2x when the family is in its top-tercile flow state (dip_buy additionally requires the 10d-MA 63d dial below 50). Posterior multipliers 1.15 / 1.23 / 1.33; 1.2 is the shrunk common value. No down-size leg: the 0.75x low-flow half costs 5.7% of PnL for 9.1% less risk and is a variance rule the brief does not need. Up-only replay 2010 to 2026 at 1.25x: book PnL +5.0% at +4.2% risk, Sharpe flat, maxDD +10%, 17 of 17 years better.

The gate. Both switch off for the day when the margin guard projects the requirement above 60% of live NLV, because the days they fire are the days that set the wall. Reliefs take the maximum with any other relief, never the product. Post-ship review written now: PnL per unit risk on the first 60 high-flow trades at least 1.3x the concurrent low-and-mid-flow trades, else retire.

The OVS same-day cluster tilt (1.25x when five or more OVS signals stage together; avgR 0.65 vs 0.23, t 4.8, posterior 1.40 to 1.48) is real and is pre-registered as a second-stage refinement rather than shipped: short_fade cap relief already covers most of the cell, and the two 2026 cluster days lost.

9. Seasonality: what 1,012 cells said no calendar layer

You asked for strategy seasonality and times to dial size up or down. The seasonality analyst tested every strategy and the book against ten calendar dimensions (month, quarter, half-year, week of month, turn of month, opex week, day of week, holiday adjacency, earnings season, cycle year), 1,012 trade-level cells and roughly 1,500 daily-basis cells, with year-paired and episode-clustered tests and false-discovery control, then replayed every fitted table and every outside prior.

  • Zero cells survive multiple-comparison correction on year-clustered tests (minimum q 0.27); the five nominally significant cells are the false-positive count expected under the null.
  • Fitted calendar multipliers are at or below baseline out of sample in 17 of 18 rule-and-design combinations; the one positive (strategy × month, walk-forward +0.04 Sharpe, t 1.0) reverses under leave-one-year-out with maxDD 2.6 points worse.
  • Every outside prior replays negative: September 0.5x −4.5% PnL (better in 1 of 24 years); August-September 0.75x −3.9%; May-October 0.75x −11.3% with maxDD unchanged; November-April 1.25x +14% PnL but Sharpe −0.033 and maxDD −3 points; single-stock earnings-season 0.75x −7.1% (in-season trades that pass the blackouts run 0.52 to 0.64R vs 0.41 to 0.48 off-season in 14 of 15 strategies); turn-of-month, opex, holiday and day-of-week tilts within ±0.02 Sharpe.
  • Seasonal variance is flow, not risk. May-October daily sd is 63 vs 83 bps (lower in 19 of 24 summers) with mean 7.1 vs 11.6 bps; the Kelly ratio is 1.03 to 1.06. Monthly sd tracks open risk (Spearman 0.77). A vol target would add size in summer and cut it in winter, exactly backwards; January's excess variance is eight episodic years of high open risk into a selloff, which is the hedge's territory.
  • Signal flow is the seasonal variable, and it sizes as a same-day state (section 7), never as a month. December, January and June are high-flow months with average per-signal quality; no "crowded month" cell exists (the largest, OLV August 0.35 vs 0.86R on N 61, still earns +$28.8k and its 0.5x replay costs PnL).

What stays: the OVS midterm 0.75x (6 of 6 midterm years below, year-clustered t −2.0; posterior 0.89; a book-wide midterm cut is Sharpe-neutral with a worse risk-matched drawdown and is rejected), per-name earnings handling (the OLV pre-print override cell shows no deficit at +0.80R on 57 trades and should be reviewed against its original basis; the OLV deficit is post-print, a re-exam trigger at +20 trades), and two watch items with written triggers: the dip-buy family's May-October gap (−0.19R, 6 of 6 members, post-2013 only, still +0.30 R per unit risk; re-open as a September-only 0.75x appetite question only if the gap stays below −0.15 and the September dip-buy cell is at or below zero after 20 more fills, around 2029). 2026's realised edge (0.52x per unit risk) is read as the midterm base rate (1.22x the prior midterm January-to-August average), not as decay.

12. Where outside evidence and the repo disagree

  • Nagel versus the FAMILY4 bands. Outside: liquidity-provision returns and conditional Sharpe rise sharply with VIX, so a reversal book should size up in stress. Repo: FAMILY4 degrades at high dial (PIT t −1.96). Reconciliation: the dial's high zone is calm-at-highs tape, almost never high-VIX (3.9% overlap), and the repo's own regime study finds the book earns 60% more R per unit risk in 3 to 10% pullbacks and VIX 20 to 30 than at dead-calm highs, with a realised risk deployment inverted against that gradient (204 bps open risk in calm tape vs 51 in stress) because the long-hold strategies build inventory in calm tape. The right response is the OLV pullback tilt and the L1 cuts on the calm-tape inventory builders, not a VIX scalar (every vol-direct overlay is within ±0.03 Sharpe and negative on 2016+).
  • Daniel-Moskowitz versus the hedge. Outside: ex-ante beta hedges of state-dependent-beta strategies do not fix crashes. Repo: the hedge's value is the dial's drift forecast, not variance, and it is armed by a positioning state rather than realised vol. Both are respected by the 1.0x cap, the 126-day beta and the paper period.
  • Carver's 25% of backtest Kelly versus 6%. Outside practice would run this book at two to four times its size. The repo's wall is the broker, not the practitioner's floor; the plan gets to 1.25x now and 1.5x by rule, and names the two changes (futures routing, guard) that lift the wall toward the practitioner band.
  • Halloween, September, turn-of-month, opex. Outside: real in long samples, decayed on liquid US large caps post-2015, T+1 settlement (2024-05-28) re-anchored the month-end mechanism. Repo: nothing transfers. Closed.
  • Sizing pilots. Outside (Brightman, Huang-Song-Xiang, the 2026 structured-product sample): strategies launched after a strong run of their own cell decay worst, 60 to 75% of vol-adjusted return lost in the first live year. Repo: pilots capped at half their estimate, stepping up on trade count, never on calendar time.
