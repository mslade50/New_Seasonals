2. Outside research that supported the changes

Five researchers pulled the literature; each item below is mapped to the change it informed. In-repo evidence decided every rule; the outside work set priors, haircuts and the shape of what to expect live.

  • How much of the backtest is real (haircut 40%, keeps, pilots capped). McLean & Pontiff (JF 2016): predictors lose 26% out of sample, 58% post-publication. Chen & Zimmermann (RAPS 2020): publication bias alone ~12%. Falck, Rej & Thesmar (QF 2022): −43% Sharpe post-publication, recent discoveries decay more. Suhonen, Lennkh & Perez (JPM 2017): sell-side alt-beta backtests lose a median 73% of Sharpe live. Harvey & Liu (JPM 2015): nonlinear haircut, t ≈ 2 strategies cut hardest. Bailey & López de Prado (JPM 2014 deflated Sharpe; J. Risk 2012 MinTRL): with 23 years of data even 1,000 trials cannot manufacture a Sharpe of 2 from noise; 11 years separates a live Sharpe of 1.0 from 0.5, so a 1–3 year live record is a kill gate, not a re-estimation input. Ilmanen et al (AQR, JIM 2021): robust simple factors lose ~30% OOS. Jensen, Kelly & Pedersen (JF 2023): backtest rank survives even when levels shrink. Brightman/Li/Liu (RA 2015) and Huang/Song/Xiang (JFQA 2024): strategies launched after a strong run of their own cell decay worst (pilot cap). Shumway (JF 1997): missing delisting returns −30/−55% (overflow survivorship keep 0.85). Hou, Xue & Zhang (RFS 2020): microcap-driven anomalies mostly fail.
  • Kelly, fractional Kelly and the GRM step. Thorp (2006) and MacLean, Thorp & Ziemba (QF 2010): practitioners run a quarter to a half of honest Kelly; overbetting is penalised far more than underbetting; GBM drawdown formulas ignore jumps (1987). Carver, Systematic Trading (2015): size to half the backtest Sharpe then half Kelly, about 25% of backtest full-Kelly, i.e. 2–4x this book's size. Baker & McHale (Decision Analysis 2013) and Chopra & Ziemba (JPM 1993): shrink the edge, not the covariance; errors in means cost ~20x errors in covariances (why the tilt shrinks μ by keep_s). Busseti, Ryu & Boyd (J. Investing 2016): risk-constrained Kelly accepts a 10% chance of a 30% drawdown for ~25–45% of growth, and uniform fractional Kelly is dominated by cutting the worst tail-per-growth bets (why the tilt, not just GRM). Grossman & Zhou (Math. Finance 1993): drawdown-constrained Kelly costs growth ×(1−α) and only pays if the drawdown state forecasts, which here it does not.
  • Why no throttle, no vol target, no drawdown cut. Moreira & Muir (JF 2017) vs Cederburg, O'Doherty, Wang & Yan (JFE 2020) and Barroso & Detzel (JFE 2021): real-time vol management fails out of sample for everything but momentum. DeMiguel, Martin-Utrera & Uppal (JF 2024): vol-scaling the market leg loses ~0.1 Sharpe OOS while momentum doubles. Kaminski & Lo (JFM 2014): stop-loss and drawdown cuts have a negative stopping premium under a random walk and to first order under mean reversion (the book's PnL autocorrelation is negative). Man Group (2025): a 20% drawdown cut-off on good uncorrelated strategies costs ~2.2%/yr. Van Hemert et al (JPM 2020): drawdown rules detect decayed managers, they do not size known-good strategies.
  • The hedge and regime conditioning. Daniel & Moskowitz (JFE 2016): ex-ante beta hedges of state-dependent-beta strategies sell the rebound (why 1.0x max, 126d beta, paper first). Barroso & Santa-Clara (JFE 2015): sleeve-level vol scaling works for momentum-type legs (supports the 52wh exit prereg, not a book scalar). Longin & Solnik (JF 2001) and Ang & Chen (JFE 2002): correlations rise with bear trend, not vol, and most for past losers (the dip-buy cell). Nagel (RFS 2012): liquidity-provision returns and conditional Sharpe rise with VIX (supports the OLV pullback tilt and cautions the FAMILY4 bands; reconciled by the dial's high zone being calm-at-highs, not high-VIX). Collin-Dufresne & Daniel (2016): the counter-view that 5-day reversal Sharpe is unrelated to VIX. CFM "Hidden beta in the dip trade" (2024): much of dip-buy return is drift, i.e. beta.
  • Seasonality (closed). Lakonishok & Smidt (RFS 1988) and Etula et al (2020 "Dash for cash"): turn-of-month exists in long samples but has decayed post-2015 on US large caps and was re-anchored by T+1 settlement in May 2024. Stivers & Sun (2013): opex week small and fading. Bouman & Jacobsen (AER 2002) / Jacobsen & Zhang (2020) vs Dichtl & Drobetz (2015): Halloween insignificant on the S&P post-1984. Keim (JFE 1983) and Gu (2003): January small-cap effect declining and untradeable net of costs. Heston & Sadka (JFE 2008): same-month seasonality underwrites the seasonal-rank strategies you already run. So & Wang (JFE 2014): pre-earnings reversal is jump-risk compensation (supports per-name blackouts, not a season rule). Sias (FAJ 2007): momentum is quarter-end seasonal (report-only cell). Lucca & Moench (JF 2015): pre-FOMC drift gone since 2015.
  • Margin and capacity (the wall). IBKR portfolio-margin documentation: TIMS ±15% stock, +6/−8% broad index, ±10% small-cap index, 3x multiplier on leveraged ETFs, 30% concentration minimum with an undisclosed trigger, 110% initial, Soft Edge Margin expiring 15:45 ET, automated liquidation with no call, the 2020 election 35% house uplift and the January 2021 300% short requirements. Almgren et al (Risk 2005), Tóth/Bouchaud square-root law, Frazzini, Israel & Moskowitz (2018, $1.7tn of live trades: ~12–20 bps per side, small caps 2x large): impact is not a boundary anywhere near the feasible range; the derived result that impact per unit of ATR risk is volatility-invariant sets the participation thresholds. Novy-Marx & Velikov (RFS 2016) and Avramov, Chordia & Goyal (2006): reversal capacity is smallest exactly where the signal is strongest (overflow), hence the tier exclusion.

3. Gaps not addressed

  1. The adversarial verification did not run as designed. The workflow's 13 evidence studies and 4 proposals completed; the judge, synthesis, per-change refuters and completeness critic were done by me inline after the subagent spend limit hit. The refutation ledger is one reviewer's pass, not three independent lenses per change.
  2. No engine confirmation has been run. Every composed number comes from scaling daily MTM vectors; the OVS P2 cap, OLV ticker cap and cross-strategy clamp were not re-applied, fills were assumed to scale linearly, and the shipped rules are the shrunk forms (0.7x, 0.8/1.2, 1.2x, clip 0.7–1.3) while the package replay used the study values (0.5x, 0.75/1.25, 1.25x, clip 0.6–1.4). Item 1.11 exists for this reason.
  3. Margin rates are stylised. The 3x-short rate (45 vs 90%), the concentration trigger, and whether "higher of rules-based and PM" applies to this account are unknown until the what-if. Under the rules-based reading the current book is already at 99% of base on 2023-02-03. Live NLV ($632k) is from a 2026-08-18 note.
  4. No live R series exists. The 40% haircut is structural plus one partial midterm year; the post-freeze OOS window is 108 trades with a CI on the ratio of [−0.57, 1.22]. The DO fills ring loses days the harvest does not run.
  5. Point-in-time dial coverage. The PIT series ends 2026-05-07; the August 2026 episode (the live one) is scored on current weights only. PIT cures weight lookahead, not the fact that signal definitions are today's code.
  6. The guard's cost is unmeasured. It trims the book's best days (gross >100% NAV days are 15% of days and 49% of PnL); a MARGIN_TRIM engine row type is needed before it can be replayed.
  7. Flow thresholds are in-sample at the level (2026 expanding-window terciles; multipliers are out of sample). Hi-flow episodes are few for oversold_hold (20) and short_fade (26); both 2026 OVS cluster days lost.
  8. The theme envelope was not walked forward and theme membership is a judgement; it ships as display only.
  9. The 52wh tail is only diagnosed. Sharpe −2.2 on 125 active days at dial ≥50 with a 0.11 beta is an exit problem (63-day holds carried into a rolling-over tape); the exit rule is a prereg (3.4), not a study.
  10. The hedge has 13 episodes, futures margin is additive, realised beta is asymmetric (up 0.50 / down 0.27 while armed), and the per-strategy daily payload ended 2026-08-07.
  11. Overflow survivorship is scenario-based; the point-in-time overflow universe is still not built, so overflow avgR remains an upper bound.
  12. Index legs to futures is a design note only; contract granularity (MES ≈ $30k notional), session hours and a second execution path are unbuilt, and it is the precondition for anything past GRM 2.25.
  13. Basis mismatches left in place: WCDS's legacy seasonal Size_Mult in the replays; the OLV pre-earnings override's original basis unknown (the cell shows no deficit; the deficit is post-print).
  14. The 2026 "midterm base rate" reading rests on six in-sample midterm episodes; OLV and LT Trend are the two strategies it does not explain, and both receive raises under the tilt (tempered, with a 2027 Q1 re-read).
  15. The PA account is not modelled at all (below the PM minimum, Reg-T); the plan is primary-only.
  16. Nothing is built. All of this is documents and evidence; no code, config or test has changed. The appetite calls inside it (0.8/1.2, the 1.5x OLV clip, the 60/70% guard lines, 1.875 vs 2.25 now) are yours to confirm before an agent starts.

4. Phase 1 in detail

Eleven items in one release behind one engine confirmation. Preconditions: 0.1 (guard) live and 0.2 (what-if) run.

1.1 GRM 1.5 → 1.875. Growth is linear through 3x current size on every window and haircut; the margin wall on the live account sits near 1.34x on the historical peak day, so 1.25x clears it with the guard and reliefs off on guard days (84% of NLV). 2.25 follows by rule S.1.

1.2 Overflow longs excluded. OVERFLOW_RISK_OVERRIDES × 0.8 so effective bps stay put: OLV 20, LT Trend 24, St OS Sznl 32, 52wh 28 nominal (three new carriers wired like OLV's). Overflow shorts take the step. Participation ≤1% of 21d median dollar ADV (0.4% for LT Trend, St OS Sznl, Weak Close, IOB), refusal above 5%. Overflow longs carry survivorship, earn no more than their liquid twins (OLV 0.68 vs 0.97R, LT Trend 0.29 vs 0.40, St OS 0.18 vs 1.02), cost twice the impact and are already at 1–2.7% of ADV.

1.3 Allocation tilt. Multipliers as in the change list. Walk-forward 2014–2026: PnL/maxDD +20%, Sharpe 2.05 → 2.14, maxDD 12.1 → 11.4%, worst held-out year −4% vs today. The four cut own 61% of worst-day losses for 36% of PnL. Scales base bps only; tilt the earnings override too; refit each January; retirement flag if raw w < 0.6 two Januaries running; OLV and LT Trend raises re-read after 2027 Q1.

1.4 OLV ladder re-keyed. Today halves 81% of solo legs (0.48R) but also 43–49% of legs entered into 3+ open names (0.93–1.15R). New: max(recency rung, depth rung), depth counting filled plus working T+3 limits (0 → 0.5, 1–2 → 0.7, 3+ → 1.0). Replay +$125k/21y raw, +$16k at equal risk, better worst-21d. Composite clip 1.5x on the product of all OLV overlays: without it the composed levers made June 2026 the worst episode (2016+ maxDD 7.3% → 11.7%).

1.5 Weak Close and LT Trend. 0.8x solo / 1.2x adds. Weak Close adds 0.73R vs 0.21 solo (t 2.7); LT Trend depth 3+ 0.63R vs 0.22 (t 2.65). PnL per unit risk +9 to +13% with lower sleeve maxDD in both.

1.6 OVS quality tier. 0.7x when the mean of the four short-window ranks is below 94. Bottom cell 0.17R (N 392) vs 0.47R (N 878), t 5.4, LOYO 80%, holds in both tiers, both gap paths, all four eras. Win rate and expectancy move together, so not the deleted meta-labeller's trap. 0.7 is the shrunk posterior; 0.5 was the in-sample fit.

1.7 OVS path-2 cap 0.75% → 1.0%. 202 of 609 P2 trades were capped; capped 0.37R vs 0.18R uncapped; full uncapping +$49.5k/24y with a worst extra day of −$1.4k.

1.8 Index clone clamps. IOB SPY+QQQ both-fire days: correlation 0.88, 1.9x the PnL at 2.3x the sd → 0.5x each. Cross-strategy same-ticker pairs (14 since 2010, R corr 0.97) → clamp extended to every dip-buy pair. MonFri's clone days earn 3.1x at 2.1x sd and stay full.

1.9 Flow-aware cap. 375 bps for a family on its top-tercile 5d raw-candidate days, counted from the filter masks (never staged rows). The cap binds on 21% of trades and on the good ones (OVS 0.62R bound vs 0.20 unbound). Unfitted replay: +3.8% PnL, Sharpe 2.20 → 2.22, maxDD and worst-21d byte-identical. Max with any other relief; off when the guard projects >60%; never removed (×99 takes short_fade maxDD from −$44k to −$127k).

1.10 Family flow up-size. 1.2x base bps on the same days (dip_buy also needs dial <50). Top-tercile flow earns 1.7–2.7x the PnL per unit risk of the bottom tercile in every mean-reverting family (t 2.7–3.2, 82–84% of years, survives VIX/trend/vol/drawdown/dial controls). Up-only replay at 1.25x: +5.0% PnL, Sharpe flat, maxDD +10%, 17 of 17 years better. Growth lever, not a Sharpe lever. No down-size leg, no breakout carrier. Post-ship review: first 60 hi-flow trades ≥1.3x the concurrent rest, else retire.

1.11 Engine gate. Pass only if all four hold: annual PnL gain ≥ +4 pts NAV/yr at 1.875 vs today; maxDD no worse than today by >1 pt at GRM-1.5-equivalent; worst-21d no worse by >10%; the 2016+ worst episode is not the June 2026 OLV stack. On fail, fix the composition (clip, guard gate), not the multipliers.

Package reference at GRM 1.875, 2005–2026: annual PnL 33.0% of NAV (today 24.6%), Sharpe 2.16 (2.00), maxDD 12.5% (12.4%), worst day 7.3% (5.9%), worst 21 days 9.5% (9.5%). Those are upper-edge numbers (study values, not the shrunk ones).
