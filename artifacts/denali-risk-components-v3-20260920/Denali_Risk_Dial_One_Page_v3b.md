# Denali Risk Dial

The goal of the risk dial is to proactively identify good and bad trading environments—the “easy dollar” versus “hard penny” markets many seasoned traders recognize. It looks beneath index price action for signs of risk that a steady or rising market can hide.

The dial combines seven components into one score; higher readings reflect stronger combined warnings. We focus on the next 5, 10 and 21 trading days—one week, two weeks and one month. SPY tracks the S&P 500.

| Component | Why we watch it | Next 5 | Next 10 | Next 21 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Distribution Dominance | Busy sessions closing below the open outnumber those closing above it; demand may be weakening. | -0.08% | -0.36% | -0.95% |
| Defensive Leadership | Defensive businesses, more resilient in a slowdown, lead economically sensitive shares; confidence may be fading. | -0.18% | -0.46% | -2.69% |
| VIX Range Compression | VIX (expected S&P 500 volatility) trades in a tight range above 13 and its recent average; unease may be building. | +0.09% | +0.08% | +0.88% |
| Low Absorption Ratio | Sectors move separately near highs; a common shock could make them fall together. | -0.04% | -0.30% | -0.50% |
| Seasonal Rank Divergence | The calendar favors defensive shares; economically sensitive stocks have less seasonal support. | -0.29% | -0.15% | +0.44% |
| Dispersion | Large individual-stock moves cancel out in a calm index; that balance can break. | +0.19% | -0.15% | -0.62% |
| NYSE Net Highs | NYSE new lows exceed new highs on a five-session average while SPY is near its high; broad participation is weak. | -0.14% | -0.20% | +0.87% |
| All dates | Comparison for the warning rows | +0.33% | +0.65% | +1.38% |

NYSE net highs are new 52-week highs minus new 52-week lows. Since 18 September the warning reads a five-session average of that total, so a single quiet day no longer switches it on or off. It is strongest closest to SPY’s high and clears when the average returns to zero.

**Reading it:** below 20 has been most supportive; 20–40 constructive; 40–50 calls for caution; 50+ is the working warning threshold. High readings were followed by weaker returns across these three windows.

The proposed dual filter—restricting only when the dial is 50 or above **and** SPY is within 2% of its high—was replayed and not adopted; the detailed report explains why.

Component rows show returns after each warning was active. Study: 2 January 2019–18 June 2026; 1,790 common observation dates. Returns include SPY dividends, using prices through 18 September 2026. All tables use the same sample and show average returns. Dates with missing highs-and-lows data are excluded.

Historical scores are reconstructed using the model’s rules. Return windows overlap. A favorable reading still carries risk.
