# Thread: our crash detector was never a crash detector

Status: unposted
Source: scratch/ultracode_research/crisis-alpha.md + book-gaps.md
Voice: dry practitioner. "Know what your indicator measures" audience.

---

1/
We run a composite risk dial that sizes down part of the book when it
reads high. For a while we talked about it like a crash detector.

Then we measured what it actually detects. It was off for Volmageddon.
It turned off BEFORE the COVID crash. Thread on knowing what your
indicator measures:

2/
The receipts, from our own backfill:
- Feb 2018 vol spike: dial never fired (max reading ~36 of 100)
- COVID: switched OFF in early March 2020, before the main leg down
- the entire Apr-Oct 2022 bear: max reading 4.9
- on in only 2 of the book's 12 worst months since 2016

3/
So what DOES it measure? We correlated it against candidate definitions.
Spearman +0.56 vs days-since-a-5%-drawdown. NEGATIVE vs realized vol.
NEGATIVE vs VIX.

High readings mean: extended, calm, near highs, nobody hedged. It's a
complacency dial. It fires before the FIRST crack, not during the storm.

4/
That reframe changed how we use it. As a crash hedge trigger it's
worthless (crashes happen with the dial at zero, because by then the
calm is gone). As a "your dip-buys are about to get worse" signal it has
a real, measured, ugly-but-tradeable edge in exactly one family of
strategies. So that's the only place it sizes anything.

5/
The general lesson: every composite indicator is secretly a definition.
Before you act on one, correlate it against the plain-English things it
might be measuring and accept whichever one wins, even if it's less
exciting than the name you gave it.

Ours is named like a smoke alarm and behaves like a "the house has been
suspiciously quiet for six weeks" alarm. Useful. Different product.
